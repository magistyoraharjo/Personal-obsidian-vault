# Session Notes — River Ambush to Hideout Discovery

## Core Premise
The party is traveling by river barge toward **Mountain Base**, a small frontier town.  
They are escorting or pursuing a lead tied to a **sealed tube** hidden in cargo: a **route map to a long-lost cave containing Rare Metal**.

The trip is interrupted by a staged ambush by **lizardmen raiders** serving a **giant river beast**.


# Scene 1 — River Ride to Mountain Base

## Purpose
- Establish destination and job
- Let players roleplay without getting stuck
- Define party motivation
- Build stats in play
- Set tone before violence

## Opening Image
Cold river. Wet wood. Slow-moving barge. Mist hanging low over dark water.  
The **boatman** is practical, talkative, and knows enough to be useful, but not enough to overshadow the party.

## What the Boatman Explains
- They are heading to **Mountain Base**
- It is a rough frontier settlement near old mining territory
- A **miner/contact** connected to the job was supposed to meet people there
- Trouble on this river has been increasing
- Something valuable is moving through the region, and scavengers know it

## Boatman as Interviewer
Use the boatman to pull answers out of the party.

### Ask these directly
- “So which one of you is actually in charge here?”
- “What’s the real job? Escort? Recovery? Investigation?”
- “What are you hoping to find in Mountain Base?”
- “What’s the one thing that would ruin this trip?”
- “If trouble starts, what matters more: the cargo, the client, or each other?”
- “Which one of you has done this sort of work before?”

## Player-Friendly Prompt Options
If players freeze, give choices.

### Goal
Choose one:
- Recover a missing miner
- Deliver valuable cargo safely
- Find the source of attacks on the river
- Reach Mountain Base and secure a buyer/contact
- Track down a map or lead tied to Rare Metal

### Fear
Choose one:
- The cargo has already been compromised
- Someone on the route sold them out
- The river is more dangerous than the raiders
- The miner/contact is already dead
- Rival scavengers are closer than they think

### What Matters Most
Choose one:
- Protect innocent lives
- Keep the cargo out of enemy hands
- Get paid no matter what
- Discover the mine at any cost
- Keep the party together

## In-Play Stat Assignment
As players answer or act, assign stats based on what they emphasize.

### Fast assignment method
- **Might / Strength**: force, endurance, aggression, hauling, intimidation
- **Agility / Dexterity**: balance, aim, reflex, stealth, climbing
- **Wits / Intelligence**: deduction, memory, planning, analysis
- **Presence / Charisma**: leadership, reading people, confidence, persuasion

### Rule
When a player solves something in a way that strongly expresses a stat, lock in a **+1** or their starting bonus there.
use +3, +2, +1, +1, 0, -1

Examples:
- Takes command of the conversation → Presence
- Notices river hazards or inconsistencies → Wits
- Moves easily on the rocking barge → Agility
- Helps with ropes, poles, cargo, or muscle work → Might


When a player uses a weapon class give them either proficiency(+2) or familiar
(+1) once each

When a player uses Magic assign one of the magic alignment to them
- divine - blessing, protection, healing, judgment, sacred light
- arcane - study, force, illusion, control, elemental shaping
- primal - nature, beasts, storms, growth, instinct
- occult - shadows, whispers, curses, mind, omen, strange unseen forces

what to give: 
- 1 manifested spell/effect
- 1 cantrip level

## GM Twist
Pick one:
- The boatman unknowingly transported the ambushers before
- The cargo holds decoy crates and one real target
- The miner never sent for help; someone else did
- The route map is only one half of the full directions
- The giant river beast is not fully under the raiders’ control

## Transition
The river bends. Something large is lodged ahead.

# Scene 2 — The Wrecked Boat

## Purpose
- Shift from calm to dread
- Let players investigate
- Seed evidence of the ambush
- Give more character expression before combat

## Opening Image
A boat lies **turned over across the river**, blocking passage.  
The hull is broken. Cargo is scattered on one bank, all crates **opened and rifled through**.  
There is **blood** on the wood and along the muddy edge.

## What Is True
- This was a deliberate attack
- The attackers found what they wanted quickly
- Some blood belongs to the original victims
- Some signs were left on purpose to draw attention and delay response
- The ambushers are still nearby

## Investigation Opportunities
Let each player do something concrete.

### Things to inspect
- Hull damage
- Cargo remains
- Blood pattern
- Riverbank tracks
- Rope cuts / hook marks
- Signs of survivors or dragged bodies

### Example discoveries
On success, reveal one strong fact. On failure, reveal a weaker or incomplete version.

- **Hull damage** → “The boat was forced and pinned, not sunk by accident.”
- **Cargo** → “Most crates were opened fast and carelessly. They were searching for something specific.”
- **Blood** → “The dead were overwhelmed quickly. Little sign of a prolonged fight.”
- **Tracks** → “Clawed feet. Several raiders. One heavier set.”
- **Deck edge / rail** → “Hook marks. Attackers climbed up from below or from the water.”
- **Missing item pattern** → “Only one object or container seems truly important.”

## Tension Clock
After the party spends enough time investigating, trigger the ambush.  
Do not wait for them to finish every possible check.

## Transition
A sharp sound.  
The **boatman is shot dead instantly**.

---

# Scene 3 — Ambush on the River

## Purpose
- Hit hard and fast
- Reveal the real enemy
- Start combat with urgency
- Establish the MacGuffin immediately

## Ambush Trigger
The boatman is killed in one sudden hit.  
Immediately after, **hooks slam onto the barge deck from beneath the overturned boat**.  
**Lizardmen bandits** rise from cover and rush aboard.

## Enemy Truth
These are not disciplined soldiers.  
They are **local river raiders** who serve a **giant crocodilian river beast** and pay it with meat.  
They were hired or directed to seize one item from the cargo.

## Raider Objective
Primary:
- Kill everyone on the barge

Secondary:
- Recover the **sealed tube** containing the route map

Tertiary:
- Spare one useful captive if someone looks especially knowledgeable

## Immediate Reveal
Very early in the fight, show that the raiders have already found the important item.

### Scripted beat
- A lizardman leader or scavenger pulls a **sealed tube** from broken cargo
- He hisses a signal
- He throws it to the riverbank
- Another raider catches it and runs toward the hideout

This clarifies:
- The party failed to keep the item secure
- The next phase is pursuit
- Not every enemy must be killed for the scene to matter

## Combat Features
Use simple, vivid terrain.

### On the barge
- Slippery deck
- Loose ropes
- Cargo for half cover
- River on both sides
- Hooks and lines hanging over the rail

### Enemy traits
- Fast climbing
- Ambush advantage on first rush
- One or two use javelins/bows
- One larger brute or scarred veteran if needed

## River Beast Presence
Use as threat, not necessarily full combatant yet.

### Signs
- Huge shape beneath water
- Barge jolts from below
- Eyes or ridged back visible for a moment
- Splash near anyone falling overboard

## End State of Scene
By the end:
- Boatman is dead
- Party survives and wins or drives off attackers
- One enemy escaped with the tube/map
- The direction of the hideout is now the obvious next move

# Break — Character Finalization

## Timing
Take a **10-minute break** after the ambush.

## Purpose
Permanently fix characters now that play has shown who they are.

## Lock In
- Final stat bonuses
- Weapons or style
- Core role
- Signature item or approach
- Any quick bond or motive formed during the ambush

Use what happened in play, not what was planned before.

Examples:
- The one who took charge becomes the leader-type
- The investigator becomes the scout or thinker
- The brutal one becomes frontliner
- The one obsessed with the map becomes seeker/explorer

---

# Scene 4 — Tracking the Raiders

## Purpose
- Move from combat to pursuit
- Provide a structured discovery sequence
- Feed intel before the hideout
- Let failures still move the game forward

## Structure
Run as **4 discovery beats**.  
Each beat gives forward motion.  
Success gives extra intel. Failure gives lesser intel, complication, or partial truth.

---

## Discovery Beat 1 — Fresh Trail

### What they see
- Clawed footprints still clear in wet ground
- Bent vegetation
- Blood from a wounded raider
- Signs of haste

### Gain on success
- Accurate estimate of **enemy numbers**
- They know how many likely remain ahead

### On failure
- They still find the trail
- But they underestimate or overestimate enemy numbers
- Or they miss that one set of prints is much larger

---

## Discovery Beat 2 — Split Path

### What they see
- Trail splits in two
- Blood trail goes **right**
- Both directions show clawed tracks
- One trail has **larger prints**

### Gain on success
- Find a **dropped map of the hideout**
- It shows basic layout or at least entry points

### On failure
- They still find the map
- But it is **ripped, muddy, or incomplete**
- Missing one key detail such as trap area, guard route, or side entrance

---

## Discovery Beat 3 — Signs of the Lair

### What they notice
- Thin column of fire smoke
- Signs of a trap nearby
- Faint whispering or hissing voices
- Smell of wet scales, rot, smoke, and river mud

### Gain on success
- Learn there is a **big guard** or heavy threat at the hideout

### On failure
- Learn there is a **prisoner** or captive inside instead
- They miss the big guard until later

---

## Discovery Beat 4 — Visual on the Hideout

### What they see
- Hideout entrance ahead
- Guards roaming outside
- The escaped lizardmen bringing a **crate from the stolen cargo** inside
- Cave mouth built into limestone and river edge

### End of scene
Stop when the party has enough to choose an approach.

---

# Scene 5 — Session End Decision Point

## End Here
End the session once the party decides **how they will approach the hideout**.

That gives the next session a clean starting action.

## Final Prompt to Players
- “What’s the plan?”
- “Fast, quiet, or loud?”
- “Who goes first?”
- “What are you trying to achieve in the first minute?”

---

# Hideout Overview

## Identity
A **half-flooded river cave** once used by a **giant crocodilian predator** as its den.  
Now it has been adapted by lizardmen into:
- raider cache
- ambush staging point
- holding area for loot and prisoners

## Atmosphere
- Wet limestone
- Shallow pools
- Mud and reed mats
- Crates and scavenged gear
- Bone piles
- Smoke drifting through the cave
- Crocodile presence everywhere

---

# Hideout Entry Options

## 1. Front Door
### Description
Direct approach through the main cave entrance.

### Pros
- Fast
- Simple
- Easy to coordinate

### Cons
- Most visible
- Most defended
- Likely immediate fight

---

## 2. High Lookout Entry
### Description
A higher access point along the limestone wall that connects deeper into the cave.

### Access
Reachable by risky movement, climbing, swinging, or acrobatic traversal.

### Pros
- Better infiltration angle
- Possible surprise
- Good for scout or split entry

### Cons
- Fall risk
- Slower setup
- Can separate party

---

## 3. Smoke Them Out
### Description
Burn the boats or river staging area outside and create noise and panic.

### Pros
- Forces movement
- Disrupts guards
- May separate weak enemies from strong ones

### Cons
- Loses stealth
- May endanger prisoner or cargo
- Could draw out the giant crocodile

---

# Key NPC / Faction Notes

## Boatman
- Friendly, plainspoken, practical
- Exists to frame the mission and make the world feel lived in
- Dies abruptly to prove stakes

## Lizardmen Raiders
- Local, brutal, opportunistic
- Know the river well
- Loyal primarily because the river beast protects them or terrorizes them

## Giant River Beast
- Huge crocodilian predator
- Fed with meat by the raiders
- Could be sacred, feared, or merely managed
- Best used as looming pressure before becoming a full encounter

---

# GM Running Notes

## What the players must understand by session end
- The sealed tube/map matters
- The raiders already took it
- The hideout is close
- There may be survivors or prisoners
- They have multiple valid ways in

## What not to overcomplicate
- Exact lore of Rare Metal
- Full politics of Mountain Base
- Detailed raider hierarchy

## What to emphasize
- Forward motion
- Clear clues
- Strong visual transitions
- Character identity emerging through action
- Every scene ends with an obvious next step

---

# One-Line Scene Flow

1. **River ride** — boatman interviews party, mission and stats take shape  
2. **Wreck ahead** — overturned boat, blood, opened cargo, signs of attack  
3. **Ambush** — boatman dies, lizardmen strike, sealed tube stolen toward hideout  
4. **Break** — finalize characters from what happened in play  
5. **Tracking** — 4 discovery beats reveal trail, split path, hideout signs, visual on cave  
6. **Decision point** — party chooses infiltration plan, session ends

# Minimal GM Cheat Sheet

## Goal
Reach the hideout and recover the stolen sealed tube/map.

## Fear
The raiders get deeper into the cave, reinforce, or destroy the clue.

## What matters most
Choose one and keep pressure on it:
- the map
- a prisoner
- the party’s survival
- keeping the discovery secret from rivals

## Twist
Pick one:
- map is incomplete
- prisoner knows the rest
- giant croc guards the deeper path
- Mountain Base has already been infiltrated
- raiders were hired by someone in town
